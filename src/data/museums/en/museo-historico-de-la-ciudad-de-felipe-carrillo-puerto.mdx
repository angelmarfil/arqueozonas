## Description {#description}

Opened on September 7, 2024, the Felipe Carrillo Puerto City Historical Museum offers insight into the history and culture of the Maya people of Quintana Roo. Its exhibits explain the Caste War and the founding of Santa Cruz, the importance of chewing gum to the regional economy, the changes brought about by education, agricultural practices, and meliponiculture, as well as artistic and religious expressions. The museum demonstrates that Mayan culture is not solely a thing of the past but remains present in local communities and is an integral part of Felipe Carrillo Puerto’s identity.

## Historical overview {#history}

The museum is housed in a historic building that originally served as an indigenous boarding school during the presidency of Lázaro Cárdenas del Río. The building itself is part of the heritage that the museum seeks to preserve, so the experience of visiting the museum begins with the building that houses it. The building allows visitors to draw a connection between the historic architecture and the exhibits presented within its spaces.

The museum’s exhibition focuses primarily on the history of the city and the region. Its content spans different historical periods and offers insight into the transformation of Felipe Carrillo Puerto over time. The museum’s curatorial approach seeks to present history from a perspective in which Maya communities occupy a central role as protagonists of historical processes.

One of the museum’s key focuses is the preservation of the history associated with Santa Cruz Báalam Naj, the city’s former name and a historic center of great importance to the region. Building on this background, the museum presents information on the city’s development and the events that shaped its history.

Prominent among its exhibits is the history of the Caste War, a conflict that profoundly influenced the region’s historical development. The museum offers insight into this period through a perspective centered on the Maya people and the social, cultural, and political circumstances that defined that era.

It also highlights aspects of the region’s economic and social life, including the importance of chewing gum production. This topic is part of the historical legacy of Felipe Carrillo Puerto and helps visitors understand some of the activities that, for decades, were intertwined with the lives of the region’s inhabitants and the use of the jungle’s resources.

Another important aspect of the museum’s mission is the preservation of Mayan cultural memory. The museum recognizes the importance of the traditions, knowledge, and cultural expressions that continue to be part of community life. In this way, the museum does not present Mayan culture solely as a thing of the past, but as a living culture that remains present today.

The exhibition design aims to bring this content to the public in an accessible way. The objects, documents, images, and other resources used in the exhibition help build a comprehensive overview of the city’s history. Visitors can explore the space by following different moments and aspects of local history, establishing connections between the past and the present.

The museum also helps strengthen local identity. By showcasing events and cultural expressions directly related to the community, it offers residents a space to recognize part of their own history. For visitors from outside the area, it provides an opportunity to better understand the city’s historical and cultural significance.

The value of the Felipe Carrillo Puerto City Historical Museum does not depend solely on the artifacts it houses. It also lies in the historic building itself, in the memory it represents, and in the role it plays as a cultural institution. The museum brings together tangible and intangible heritage and offers a space to reflect on the history of the city and its inhabitants.

Its location within a historic building reinforces the institution’s character. The building, in addition to serving as the museum’s home, is part of the history the museum seeks to convey. This allows visitors to engage with both the architecture and the historical content found within.

Taken as a whole, the museum represents a space where the past of Felipe Carrillo Puerto can be explored, interpreted, and appreciated. Its existence helps strengthen the community’s cultural identity and keep alive the historical memory of a region whose Maya heritage is one of its fundamental elements.
