## Description {#description}

The venue hosts exhibitions related to the building’s history and the region’s artistic heritage. Its galleries feature paintings, sculptures, religious objects, liturgical items, gold and silverwork, and historical documents from the 17th, 18th, and 19th centuries, as well as materials illustrating the various stages of construction and transformation of the former convent.

## Historical overview {#history}

The hall is located in the former Convent of St. Francis of Assisi, in the city of Pachuca de Soto, Hidalgo. This property is one of the state’s most iconic historic buildings, due to its architectural and religious significance. Its construction is linked to the process of evangelization carried out during the viceregal period, when religious orders established convents that served as centers for education, social organization, and the spread of religion. Over time, the building has undergone various transformations, but it retains elements that offer insight into part of the region’s colonial history.

Its exhibition spaces feature permanent collections and temporary exhibits that showcase the diversity of Hidalgo’s cultural heritage. The exhibitions include historical objects, religious artifacts, paintings, sculptures, liturgical items, documents, and other cultural artifacts that reflect the social, artistic, and religious practices of different eras. Through these pieces, visitors can learn about the way of life, beliefs, and artistic expressions of the communities that have been part of Hidalgo’s history.

One of the most important aspects of the Exhibition Hall at the INAH Hidalgo Center is its role as a space for cultural outreach. It not only preserves ancient artifacts but also creates opportunities for students, researchers, and the general public to engage directly with the region’s historical heritage. Through guided tours, exhibitions, and educational activities, the venue promotes the appreciation and protection of the cultural assets that form part of the local population’s identity.

The space also helps raise awareness of the importance of the former convent’s architectural heritage. The building’s structure, its decorative elements, and its connection to the history of Pachuca provide insight into the social and cultural changes that have taken place in the region over the centuries. A visit to the exhibition hall offers an opportunity to observe how architecture, art, and history intertwine to preserve collective memory.

In addition to its historical value, the Exhibition Hall at the INAH Hidalgo Center serves as a meeting point between the past and the present. Its activities aim to foster interest in culture and encourage community participation in heritage preservation. Each exhibition offers new perspectives on the history of Hidalgo and highlights the importance of keeping alive the material evidence that illustrates the development of society.
