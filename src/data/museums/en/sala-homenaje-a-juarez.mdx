## Description {#description}

The gallery in Guelatao, the Zapotec village where President Juárez was born, tells the story of his life with a selection of personal possessions, biographical and historical materials relating to the difficult period of Mexican history in which he lived.

## Historical overview {#history}

The Juárez Tribute Hall is a museum and cultural space dedicated to preserving and promoting the life, work, and legacy of Benito Juárez García, one of the most significant figures in Mexican history. It is located in Guelatao de Juárez, Oaxaca—the place where he was born on March 21, 1806—and is a site of great historical importance, allowing visitors to gain insight into the events that shaped his upbringing, his political career, and his contribution to the strengthening of the Mexican state.

The permanent exhibition brings together a wide variety of historical materials, including photographs, engravings, reproductions of official documents, bibliographic works, objects related to Juárez’s life, and museum displays that illustrate the most significant moments of his life. Through these exhibits, visitors can learn about his childhood in the Sierra Norte of Oaxaca, his academic education, his career as a lawyer, legislator, and governor, as well as his role as president of the Republic during one of the country’s most challenging periods.

The exhibition also explores the historical context of the 19th century, including the political and social reforms implemented during his administration—known as the Reform Laws—which promoted the separation of church and state, the consolidation of the secular state, and the legal equality of citizens. It also explains his leadership during the French Intervention and his defense of national sovereignty against the Second Mexican Empire—events that cemented his status as a symbol of the rule of law and republican resistance.

In addition to its historical significance, the Juárez Tribute Room serves an important educational and cultural function. Through guided tours, outreach activities, and exhibitions, it fosters an understanding of the nation’s historical heritage and encourages reflection on the civic values associated with Benito Juárez, such as justice, freedom, respect for the rule of law, and equality before the law. The venue serves as a meeting place for students, researchers, tourists, and the general public interested in understanding the significance of one of the key figures in Mexican history.

Due to its location in Guelatao de Juárez, the hall is part of a complex of historic sites that commemorate the origins and life of the “Benemérito de las Américas.” A visit here offers the opportunity to learn not only about Benito Juárez’s political legacy, but also about the cultural and natural environment that shaped him, making it a space for historical remembrance and appreciation of the cultural heritage of Oaxaca and Mexico.
